# Still life and material

Apply the shared philosophy to food, flowers, objects, and surfaces. These are practical applications of the shared principles, not a newly researched historical genre survey.

Find the relationship that makes the material tangible: translucent petals around a warm centre, pale food against ceramic, wet fruit beside a dry counter, or warm wood beneath blue room light. Preserve differences among materials rather than making every surface equally bright, saturated, or textured.

Vivid colour can be the photograph's strongest existing quality. When the user prefers the baseline's colour, identify what the edit lost in hue, intensity or luminosity before calling the result more refined. Preserve useful cleanup while restoring that quality; this does not imply that every flower needs stronger colour.

Subject separation is a relationship, not a brightness target. Brightening petals and foliage together can make a flower easier to see while flattening its depth. Judge the petal folds, edges and surrounding light together at the intended viewing size. Strengthen the useful distinction without making the flower appear independently lit or erasing the setting.

A familiar object does not establish neutral illumination. Food can become readable within coloured ambience without turning the room into daylight. Judge the object and the light source together; correcting the entire frame for one plate may destroy the colour contrast that gives the encounter its character. Conversely, preserving ambience is insufficient when the food remains an unreadable dark patch.

Distinguish luminous material from specular reflection. Pale petals need gentle transitions; glossy glaze and wet food can carry small brilliant reflections. Suppressing every bright point can make the material dull without revealing new detail. Inspect what the highlight actually describes before deciding whether it is an obstacle.

Softness may describe depth, translucency, or a capture limitation. Do not turn a delicate flower into brittle edges to prove that an edit occurred. A balanced, already-rendered meal can need almost no intervention. For a weak source, separate the best defensible treatment from the stronger photograph that would require a different capture.
