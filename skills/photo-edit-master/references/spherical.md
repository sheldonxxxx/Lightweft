# Spherical photographs and selected views

Apply these principles alongside the scene's genre guidance. A sphere and a conventional photograph selected from it can share a source while making different claims about the encounter. Decide which experience each output should support before choosing its emphasis.

## A sphere is a place to explore

Choose an inviting opening direction, but do not treat the rest of the sphere as expendable background. A viewer may turn away from the first subject. Look for coherence among the surrounding spaces and for worthwhile relationships in more than one direction. Distributed attention can be the point; every direction need not contain an equally strong subject.

Read the photograph as surrounding space, not solely as its flattened map. The map's boundaries are neighbours in the scene, and its apparent stretching near the top and bottom does not by itself establish a defect in the immersive view. Judge the visible horizon, joined regions, overhead space, and ground as the viewer encounters them. An attractive forward view does not establish that the sphere works as a whole.

Light can differ substantially around a place. Preserve the connection between direct light, shade, reflections, and the illuminated sky rather than forcing all directions toward equal brightness or colour. A correction should continue convincingly through adjoining regions. Visible bands, sudden changes in colour, or a local emphasis that becomes conspicuous when the viewer turns weaken that continuity. Check whether stronger clarity makes stitching errors or close objects more distracting; tonal improvement cannot repair absent or misaligned scene structure.

## A selected view is an authored frame

Choose the direction that expresses the strongest supported relationship, then decide how much surrounding space it needs. A striking object may matter less than its relation to a person, route, reflection, or distant land. Compare plausible views when they reveal that tradeoff; do not equate the default direction, the widest view, or a novel projection with the most meaningful photograph.

Judge the actual conventional view before accepting the composition. A promising region in the flattened sphere may become awkward when presented as a normal photograph. The chosen breadth changes the weight of foreground and distance and the appearance of objects near the edges. Keep faces, bodies, architecture, and close objects convincing for the intended photograph. A tighter view may simplify the story while losing the scale or context that made it worth telling.

Let the chosen frame lead to a fresh edit judgment. The sphere's balanced surroundings may become a distracting edge in a conventional view, while a subtle figure may now carry the image. Preserve the captured light and context while reassessing emphasis. Do not assume that the best treatment of the sphere is also the best treatment of every selected view.

## Judge each delivered experience

Compare processing methods through the same captured relationships. Keep exposure count, viewpoint, subject scale and viewing conditions comparable, and distinguish a benefit of combining exposures from a benefit of retaining a richer source. A brighter default rendering can look more inviting without retaining more detail; judge each method after a credible treatment aimed at the same intention. Look for an actual gain in illuminated texture, shaded material, colour relationships or atmosphere, and name any cost in noise, motion or continuity. More stored information offers editing possibilities, not an automatic aesthetic victory.

For the sphere, assess the opening view and continuity through the surrounding place. For the conventional photograph, assess the frame's meaning, proportions, edges, and intended viewing size. A more concentrated view can make source softness, noise, or stitching defects more intrusive without creating new detail. Name that cost separately from the composition's gain. A source can remain useful as an explorable record while yielding no convincing detailed conventional photograph; angle selection does not repair that limit. Neither an appealing selected view nor a technically valid spherical export establishes the quality of the other output.
