# Collection judgment

Use when selecting or editing a photo book, essay, or successive batches from a large archive. The strongest collection is not necessarily the set of individually loudest photographs.

## Start with what the collection already says

Review the current accepted photographs together before extending the selection. Keep unresolved candidates and photographs on hold identifiable; they can reveal a possible direction without becoming settled anchors. Read the actual pictures alongside their decisions, since titles and species counts do not establish visual variety.

Identify both strengths and gaps. Consider subject and species, observable behaviour, place and habitat, subject scale, light and colour, graphic structure, orientation and emotional pace. Equal representation is not required. A trip dominated by one encounter can remain so, but repeated portraits or wing positions should earn their space through a meaningful difference.

Choose a useful contribution for each proposed addition: an opening sense of place, a decisive encounter, a quieter pause, an interaction, a material detail, a transition or a closing image. These are possible functions, not a mandatory chapter template. Name what the photograph adds and what existing image it risks repeating. A beautiful frame may belong in reserve when it adds little to the book; a quieter environmental frame may be essential.

## Find the moment without mistaking sampling for a cull

Use chronological capture groups to navigate a large archive, while checking that they actually describe one visual episode. A midpoint can miss a brief interaction or a change of scene. For unexplored groups relevant to a gap, inspect the beginning and end as well as the middle, and additional moments in long or changing groups. Expand promising episodes before choosing their representative frame. Do not label unseen frames rejected.

Within an episode, compare readable behaviour, body and wing geometry, subject overlap, gaze, contact with the setting, light and potential cropping. Then inspect the detail that could defeat the intended use. Retain a neighbouring alternative if expression and usable detail compete. Sharpness alone should not displace an intelligible gesture, and rarity alone should not excuse a photograph that cannot communicate it.

Edit potential needs a credible path from the actual source: identify what framing, tone, colour or distraction control can improve and what remains constrained by focus, motion, clipping or obstruction. Do not justify a weak selection by assuming missing anatomy, hidden behaviour or absent detail can be reconstructed. A camera-rendered preview supports selection; it does not prove RAW highlight recovery or finished print quality.

## Judge rhythm and page relationships

Place proposed additions among the established photographs and read the sequence at a consistent viewing size. Look for changes in scale and visual intensity, repeated silhouettes, abrupt colour jumps and too many competing peaks. Pair images through a legible relationship—gesture, place, light, form, contrast or progression—rather than species alone.

Consider useful page shapes and breathing room. A wide habitat view can establish scale or bridge two close encounters; a portrait or detail can slow the pace. Preserve room for movement and avoid planning a binding gutter through a critical face, contact point or gesture. Treat spread ideas as provisional until the book's dimensions and layout are known. Do not reject a strong frame merely to fill an arbitrary orientation quota.

Consistency should live in the treatment of light, colour, material and attention, while allowing the scene to change. An airy backlit moment need not match the brightness of a dark woodland portrait. A useful shared style supports both; matching every histogram or forcing one dramatic grade can flatten the book's rhythm.

For a next batch, propose a manageable set that addresses the clearest gaps and retains individual photographic strength. Explain its contribution, intended edit and source limit. Keep worthwhile but redundant alternatives in reserve, and state the scope of the visual pass honestly.
