# Learning and applying taste

Learning should change the next edit. A profile that describes preferences but does not affect rendered decisions has not completed that work. Use this loop for revisions and new photographs alike, alongside image-specific judgment and the editor's execution workflow.

## Ground preferences in evidence

Read the current brief and relevant review comments before editing. Reconcile each decision with its exact rendered version. Retain stable photograph and variant IDs, asset revision or file hash, feedback text, decision, and the accepted comparison where available. Conversation endorsements are valid evidence; identify them as such instead of inventing review checkmarks. Selection approval, artistic approval, rendered acceptance, and book inclusion are different decisions. An explicit hold or removal controls inclusion without erasing historical feedback.

Inspect the referenced pixels. Record what the photographer valued or rejected as a visible relationship: for example, a pale subject against a deeper environment, readable small colour accents, or darkness that preserves the time of day. Do not infer a universal palette from one liked photograph or treat every feature of a supplied preset as endorsed.

For each consequential preference, retain compact fields in the private workspace profile or a linked evidence file:

- **Evidence:** exact comments and relevant accepted or rejected renders, with provenance.
- **Meaning and scope:** the visual relationship to reproduce and the subjects or lighting conditions for which it is supported.
- **Confidence:** explicit preference, repeated correction, supported interpretation, or untested hypothesis; include conflicts and counterexamples.
- **Action and limit:** what to decide differently, what to preserve, and a visible sign of excess.

Keep this proportional to the work. Link existing review records rather than duplicating every comment. Keep personal photographs, quotations, recipes, and taste profiles outside shared skill files.

## Turn feedback into changed decisions

Current explicit instructions control. Next use accepted examples and repeated corrections relevant to this image, then broader supported preferences, then general editorial judgment. A specific request for a brighter photograph can coexist with darkness in twilight scenes. A request to try monochrome on one image does not establish a monochrome collection style.

Distinguish three causes of a miss:

- The preference was unknown: add evidence and test the interpretation.
- The preference was known but the operation contradicted it: correct the decision, not merely the profile wording.
- The operation was sensible but the render failed: revise the execution and inspect again.

A repeated correction is a required check for the next applicable image. Compare it with the previous plan and actual recipe to locate the contradiction. Do not make the same global change more cautiously when the user asked for a different spatial relationship. Do not call a preference learned merely because it was saved.

## Apply before delivering

Select one or two accepted visual anchors that match the important relationship, not just the species or colour name. Where available, keep a rejected comparison showing the known failure. For a new scene type, transfer only supported relationships and keep the new interpretation provisional.

Before rendering, make a short image-specific decision record:

1. What should draw attention, and what role does this photograph serve?
2. Which relevant preference and visible anchor support the choice?
3. How should subject, foreground, distance, and sky differ in light and colour?
4. What previously rejected appearance must be absent, and what accepted quality must survive?

Translate these aims into the editor's native operations. A profile does not supply universal exposure, colour, crop, mask, blur, or denoise values. Different photographs can require opposite operations to achieve the same relationship. Preserve editable versions and source integrity through the execution skill.

Render, then make two distinct judgments:

- **Taste:** At the intended viewing size, compare the result with the brief, accepted anchor, and known miss. Name the visible gain. Requested drama, separation, darkness, or quietness must be apparent in pixels; a long edit history or matching colour label is not evidence.
- **Technical quality:** Inspect native detail and changed mask boundaries for texture loss, noise amplification, halos, discontinuities, or damaged retained content. Recheck the whole image after a local repair.

If a known applicable miss remains, revise internally before delivery. If fulfilling the preference demonstrably damages the image, retain the stronger version and explain that specific tradeoff rather than silently neutralising the style. Do not report an unperformed comparison or a passed technical check as proof of taste alignment.

“One shot” means one well-supported first candidate delivered to the user after internal experimentation and verification. It does not mean one tool call, an uninspected preset, or guaranteed approval. Ask only when a consequential uncertainty cannot be resolved from existing evidence; do not repeatedly ask the photographer to restate established taste.

## Check whether learning transfers

After a rule changes, replay the decision against a relevant accepted and rejected pair, where available. Check that it explains the preference without requiring every image to look alike. This is a retrospective check, not proof of performance on unseen photographs.

Use the next applicable new photograph or batch to evaluate transfer. Preserve the first delivered rendition and the profile version used. After review, distinguish accepted first deliveries, artistic revisions, selection removals, holds, and unanswered cases. Report counts only from explicit decisions; do not score an unreviewed edit as accepted or fold source-selection rejection into artistic acceptance. Small samples and retrospective checks cannot establish a reliable success rate.

When the same defect recurs, record the recurrence, inspect why the pre-delivery comparison missed it, and change the decision or checking process. Keep a new preference provisional until supported, and retain old accepted references when a later experiment fails. The profile should grow more useful and more selective, not merely longer.
